## Interpretation and counting rules

The manuscript's Tables 6 and 7 record **publication-level literature presence**, with overlapping mechanism or metric categories. They should not be read as implemented-only or measured-only counts.

The additional evidence layer distinguishes:

- **Mechanisms:** implemented, evaluated, proposed, discussed, or unclear / insufficiently established.
- **Metrics:** measured, pilot/screening or otherwise phase-qualified, weakly documented, planned, cited/discussed, or unclear.

Source support and phase, construct, and attribution qualifications matter. An unsupported-as-coded label means that examined evidence did not establish that exact classification; it does not prove the underlying activity never occurred.

Duplicate or synonymous claim labels are reconciled by **publication and family** before producing frequencies. The workbook's stricter evidence-status counts remain **provisional** where source or classification uncertainty persists.

### Usability clarification

The original broad SUS/usability family comprises **37 coded publications**. An instrument-level audit identified **three** publications documenting administration of the standard ten-item System Usability Scale (R0002, R0029, and R0073). This does not mean that only three publications discussed or assessed usability by any method.

## Scope and publication caveats

The Round 14 workbook is a supplementary quality-control and interpretation artifact, not a separate systematic review, independent validation experiment, or a replacement for the original 51-study master workbook.

Before reproducing quotations or excerpts from cited articles, check the relevant publication rights. The workbook includes qualified and unresolved decisions; users should not treat every claim-level label as independently established by complete full-text re-verification.
